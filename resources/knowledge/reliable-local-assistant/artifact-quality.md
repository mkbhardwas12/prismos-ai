# Acceptance checks for executive presentations and business documents

Reviewed: 2026-09-08. Classification: public reference; original editorial and engineering guidance.

An executive deck should communicate the decision requested, business objective, scope, staged approach, key risks, governance, acceptance gates and unresolved dependencies. Prefer short slides with detail in notes or an appendix. Label estimates and assumptions. Technical specificity is valuable only when supported by applicable evidence.

For a DEV, Test, Stage and Prod rollout, a generic planning pattern is to validate the method in DEV, test functionality and integrations in Test, rehearse operational readiness in Stage, and require an explicit go/no-go before Prod. This is a planning example, not a vendor-certified sequence or a guaranteed downtime estimate. Owners, dates and measured rehearsal results must come from the actual project.

File acceptance is separate from content acceptance. Confirm the requested format, nonempty file, valid container, readable layout, no clipped text, consistent typography, complete requested scope, usable notes/tables, source references and clear uncertainty. Word, PowerPoint, PDF and Excel are different deliverables; text describing a deck or a CSV renamed XLSX does not satisfy the request.

Failed generation should preserve the original request and source context, show the actual error, and avoid claiming completion. A successful file write still does not make its business or technical claims verified.
